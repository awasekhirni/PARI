9.	Module M09	Granular Access Control (RBAC + ABAC)
a.	Summary
the Granular Access Control system, serves as the foundational security gatekeeper for the PARI (Privacy Preserving Payment Anonymous Regulatory Insights) platform. It represents a paradigm shift from traditional, static identity management to a dynamic, context-aware, and cryptographically secure authorization framework. In a financial ecosystem where the tension between privacy (payer anonymity) and accountability (payee identification) is paramount, M09 ensures that the right entities access the right data at the right time, strictly enforcing the principle of least privilege and zero trust.

At its core, M09 implements a hybrid model combining Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) for structural hierarchy and Attribute-Based Access Control (ABAC) for granular, contextual decision-making. While RBAC efficiently manages broad functional assignments (e.g., "Cashier," "Auditor," "Compliance Officer"), ABAC introduces the necessary nuance required by international regulations, evaluating attributes such as user clearance, data sensitivity, geographical jurisdiction, time of access, and device trust score. For instance, a Tax Auditor may possess the role to view transactions, but an ABAC policy will restrict their access solely to records within their legal jurisdiction and only during business hours, unless a specific, cryptographically signed emergency override is invoked.

A distinguishing feature of M09 is its deep integration with PostgreSQL Row-Level Security (RLS). Unlike application-layer security which can be bypassed, M09 pushes enforcement down to the database layer. Security policies are compiled directly into the database query planner, ensuring that even if the application server is compromised, the database will refuse to return unauthorized rows. This "defense-in-depth" approach is critical for protecting the unlinkability of payer identities. The system utilizes advanced masking techniques, dynamically redacting PII (Personally Identifiable Information) based on the requester's privileges, ensuring that a merchant sees payment details necessary for settlement but never the payer’s legal identity.

The module is designed with a "Zero Standing Privilege" philosophy. Users rarely hold persistent high-privilege access. Instead, M09 orchestrates a Just-In-Time (JIT) access model. When an administrator needs to perform a sensitive task, they must request access, providing a business justification. The system, integrated with EU Digital Identity Wallets (eIDAS 2.0), validates the user’s identity via strong cryptographic proof (e.g., Qualified Web Authentication). Upon approval, temporary credentials are issued with a strict expiration window, automatically revoking access upon task completion.

Integration with eIDAS 2.0 transforms the module into a sovereign identity gateway. It accepts digital signatures and attributes from national identity wallets, verifying them against trusted European Member State registries. This eliminates the reliance on static passwords, replacing them with phishing-resistant, hardware-backed cryptographic assertions. Furthermore, the module features an AI-driven behavioral analysis engine that establishes a baseline of normal activity for every identity. It detects anomalies—such as impossible travel trajectories, bulk data exports from a new device, or access attempts during non-working hours—and triggers step-up authentication challenges or automatic account lockdowns.

From a governance perspective, M09 is fully CMMI Level 5 compliant. It provides immutable audit trails of every authorization decision, every policy change, and every data access attempt. This allows independent auditors to reconstruct the "state of access" at any point in the past, a requirement for anti-corruption investigations and financial forensics. By unifying rigorous access control with privacy-preserving technology, Module M09 ensures that PARI remains a fortress of trust, enabling the free flow of value while guaranteeing the integrity and confidentiality of the ecosystem.

The scope of Module M09 encompasses the entire Identity and Access Management (IAM) lifecycle within the PARI ecosystem, specifically focusing on authorization (AuthZ) and its integration with authentication (AuthN).



    In Scope:
         Design and implementation of hybrid RBAC/ABAC policy models.
         PostgreSQL RLS policy definition, enforcement, and management.
         Integration with eIDAS 2.0 European Digital Identity Wallets for strong authentication.
         Just-In-Time (JIT) and Zero Standing Privilege workflow orchestration.
         Dynamic data masking and column-level security.
         Centralized audit logging and forensic trail generation for access events.
         Administration interfaces for policy creation, simulation, and governance.
         AI/ML-driven anomaly detection for behavioral security.
         Cryptographic key management integration for session signing.



    Out of Scope:
         Core Identity Provider (IdP) functionality (e.g., user registration, password storage—which is handled by Module M05 or external IdPs).
         Network-level security (Firewalls, DDoS protection—handled by Infrastructure).
         Application-level UI rendering (handled by Module M04).
