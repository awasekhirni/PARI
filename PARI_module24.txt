

24.	Module M24	Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) Migration Layer
a.	Summary
The Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) Migration Layer (Module M24) serves as the cryptographic bedrock of the PARI ecosystem, designed to secure the next-generation payment infrastructure against the existential threat posed by quantum computing. As quantum capabilities advance, the widely deployed public-key cryptosystems—such as RSA, ECC, and Elliptic Curve Digital Signature Algorithm (ECDSA)—that underpin current financial security, digital identities, and blockchain technologies face the risk of being rendered obsolete. This vulnerability introduces the "Harvest Now, Decrypt Later" attack vector, where adversaries could store encrypted PARI transactions today to decrypt them once sufficiently powerful quantum computers exist. Module M24 neutralizes this threat by implementing a robust, crypto-agile abstraction layer that integrates NIST-standardized Post-Quantum Algorithms (e.g., CRYSTALS-Dilithium for signatures and CRYSTALS-Kyber for key encapsulation) alongside classical algorithms in a hybrid execution model.

Architecturally, Module M24 functions as a high-performance cryptographic service, interfacing with the Wallet (M01) and Exchange (M05) layers to ensure that all digital coins, blind signatures, and regulatory metadata are signed using quantum-resistant primitives. The module leverages the Open Quantum Safe (OQS) library and hardware-backed Security Modules (HSMs) to provide offloaded, high-throughput cryptographic operations without compromising latency requirements. By employing a "Hybrid Signature" approach—where a transaction is signed simultaneously with a classical key (e.g., ECDSA P-256) and a post-quantum key (e.g., Dilithium3)—PARI ensures backward compatibility during the transitional period while guaranteeing long-term security against quantum attacks. This dual-signature strategy ensures that even if one algorithm fails, the integrity of the financial transaction remains intact.

Governed by CMMI Level 5 principles, the module includes rigorous process management for cryptographic agility. It automates the lifecycle of cryptographic keys, versioning, and algorithm deprecation schedules. It features an embedded "Crypto-Benchmarking Engine" that continuously monitors the performance overhead of post-quantum operations (key generation time, signature size, verification latency) against defined Service Level Objectives (SLOs). Should a specific algorithm show signs of degradation or theoretical weakness (identified via AI-driven threat intelligence feeds), the system can dynamically initiate a migration protocol to rotate to a stronger candidate standard (e.g., moving from Dilithium to Falcon or SPHINCS+ for stateless hash-based signatures) without system downtime.

Furthermore, the PQC Layer is deeply integrated with the Compliance Hub (M02) to meet emerging regulatory standards such as the French ANSSI recommendations or the US National Security Memorandum (NSM-10) on quantum readiness. It maintains an immutable cryptographic ledger of algorithm versions used for every transaction batch, enabling auditors to verify the security posture of the system at any point in history. By decoupling the application logic from the underlying cryptographic primitives, Module M24 ensures that PARI is not just a payment system for today, but a future-proof sovereign digital utility capable of protecting the financial privacy and fiscal integrity of its stakeholders for decades to come.
b.	Scope
The scope of Module M24 encompasses the full lifecycle management of post-quantum and hybrid cryptographic primitives within the PARI infrastructure.

     Algorithm Abstraction: Providing a unified API for classical (RSA, ECC) and post-quantum (Lattice-based, Hash-based, Code-based) algorithms.
     Hybrid Execution: Orchestrating combined signing and encryption operations to ensure security against both classical and quantum adversaries.
     Key Management: Secure generation, storage (via HSM/KMS), rotation, and destruction of quantum-safe keys.
     Performance Orchestration: Load balancing cryptographic operations between CPU, GPU, and HSM accelerators to maintain sub-500ms transaction latencies.
     Compliance & Auditing: Logging all cryptographic parameters, key versions, and algorithm identifiers for regulatory reporting.
     Migration Governance: Automated workflows for sunsetting weak algorithms and deploying new ones (Crypto-Agility).

c.	Business Case
The deployment of Module M24 is driven by the critical need to protect the long-term viability of the PARI payment system and the assets of its stakeholders.

     Risk Mitigation (Harvest Now, Decrypt Later): Financial transaction data, even if anonymized via blind signatures, has a long shelf life. If encrypted data intercepted today is decrypted in 10-15 years, the financial privacy of citizens and the trade secrets of merchants could be exposed. PQC prevents data exfiltration from becoming a future liability.
     Regulatory Pre-emption: Governments and central banks are already mandating migration schedules for quantum-resistant cryptography. By integrating PQC now, PARI avoids costly, emergency retrofits and ensures immediate compliance with frameworks like eIDAS 2.0 and PSD3 future amendments.
     Competitive Differentiation: Most current fintech and blockchain solutions are "quantum-vulnerable." PARI’s proactive stance offers a unique selling proposition to institutional partners (Central Banks, Tax Authorities) who require century-long security guarantees for digital currency issuance.
     Operational Continuity: The module’s crypto-agility ensures that advances in cryptanalysis (breaking a specific algorithm) do not result in system outages. The system can patch cryptographic vulnerabilities via configuration rather than code rewrites.

d.	KPIs
     Crypto Agility Score: Time (in hours) required to onboard and activate a new cryptographic algorithm standard across the cluster.
     Migration Rehearsal Success Rate: Percentage of successful dry-run migrations (e.g., switching from Test-Algo-A to Test-Algo-B) without transaction errors.
     PQC Overhead Ratio: Percentage increase in transaction latency and payload size when using Hybrid PQC vs. Classical-only (Target: <10% latency, <20% size).
     Key Rotation Coverage: Percentage of keys successfully rotated before their cryptographic validity expiration window.
     Vulnerability Response Time: Time taken to disable a compromised algorithm across the network following a security advisory (Target: <1 hour).

End Users (Citizens)	Device Battery Life & Speed	Heavier crypto math on mobile wallets could drain battery or slow payments. Requires efficient implementation and offloading to server-side where possible.
